## Why this document matters to SRL — the most consequential of the four

- **PRECEDENCE RUNS THE OPPOSITE WAY.** TIS: *"If any terms of the CBC are found
  to be inconsistent with any terms in this Confirmation, **the terms of this
  Confirmation shall prevail**."* SRL's rate confirmation says the **BCA**
  controls on conflict. Both are defensible architectures and they are mirror
  images. TIS's choice makes the per-load document the operative instrument,
  which is arguably the more honest reading of how these documents are actually
  used at a dock. This is a genuine fork and it belongs in front of Dirk, not
  in a code comment.
- **"whether or not executed by Carrier"** — incorporation that survives an
  unsigned master agreement. Directly relevant to SRL, whose standalone BCA is
  still a first-carrier blocker.
- **Cites 49 CFR 371.3** — the broker record-keeping regulation — by number.
- **The no-appointment-time case is handled explicitly.** When the time is
  `00:00` or a range, the carrier must still arrive on the date within facility
  hours. SRL's detention clock is now void outside the appointment window, so
  SRL has an unanswered question TIS has already answered: what is the window
  when there is no appointment?
- **OS&D reporting with BOL notation.** SRL has nothing on overage, shortage or
  damage anywhere on its rate confirmation.
- **The cannot-witness-loading case.** What a driver does when the dock will not
  let him count — call, and notate before signing. This is the single most
  practical clause in the corpus and it costs one sentence.
- **Reefer temperature keyed to the BOL, with an explicit conflict procedure.**
  SRL prints a setpoint on the rate confirmation and tells the driver not to sign
  a BOL that disagrees. TIS inverts it: the BOL governs, and a conflict triggers
  written confirmation from the shipper. Worth deciding deliberately which way
  SRL wants, because SRL currently asserts the rate confirmation's number.
- **HOS as a carrier warranty**, plus advance notice of late arrival. Allen Lund
  frames the same duty generously (we will move the appointment or release you);
  TIS frames it as a warranty with customer deductions flowing back to the
  carrier's rate. Two very different postures toward the same fact.
