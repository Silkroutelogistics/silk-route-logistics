The confirmation is dated **two days after the load moved** (verbal agreement

## Incorporation + acceptance (page 1, verbatim)

> This confirmation governs the rate for the movement of the above-referenced freight as of the date specified and hereby amends, and is incorporated by reference and becomes part of that certain Agreement by and between BROKER and CARRIER. By means of either its signature on the Confirmation or its provision of service: i) CARRIER shall perform its services in accordance with all applicable laws, rules, and regulations (including, if applicable, the requirement to record hours of service via electronic logging device); and ii) CARRIER shall be conclusively presumed to have agreed to the rates and conitions set forth herein. CARRIER shall be conclusively presumed to have agreed to the rates and conditions set forth herein. **CARRIER further represents and warrants that said mutually agreed upon rates are reasonable and compensatory, that the freight would not have been tendered to CARRIER at higher rates, and that no shipments handled under such rates will subsequently be subject to a later claim of undercharges.**

Two defects in the original: "conitions" *[sic]*, and the
"conclusively presumed to have agreed" sentence is printed **twice**
consecutively.

**The undercharge warranty is the notable clause.** The carrier warrants not
merely that it accepts the rate, but that the rate is "reasonable and
compensatory," that it could not have gotten more, and that it will never bring
an undercharge claim. This is defensive drafting against the filed-rate /
undercharge litigation that followed motor-carrier deregulation. Nothing
comparable appears in any other document in this corpus.

> Carrier must be CARB compliant when traveling to, from or through California and Carrier agrees to and indemnify Echo and all other parties from any loss or damage resulting from Carrier's failure to comply.

The withholding trigger bundles **three distinct failures into one remedy**:
double-brokering, rail substitution, and co-loading. Consolidation with other
freight — i.e. the absence of exclusive use — is treated as equivalent to
double-brokering. That is a tighter construction than C.H. Robinson's separate
exclusive-use liquidated-damages clause, and it is one line rather than a
paragraph.

## Structural observations

- **A layover was negotiated into the original rate.** This is the practical
  alternative to detention-claim machinery: when the broker already knows the
  load will sit, it prices the sit up front and the carrier never files a claim.
  Every other document in the corpus treats waiting purely as a post-hoc
  reimbursement fight.
- **The undercharge warranty** is a category of protection no other document in
  the corpus carries, and it protects the broker against a claim type
  (retroactive rate challenge) rather than an operational failure.
- **Co-loading is punished as severely as double-brokering.** For a broker whose
  differentiation is exclusive-use service, folding consolidation into the
  same withholding trigger is a cheap and clear construction.
- **Insurance limits printed on the face of the document** make the confirmation
  self-contained for a carrier's own compliance check — worth considering for
  SRL, which currently states limits only in the BCA.
- The document has a signature line, a fax-back instruction, no deadline for
  paperwork, no standard payment term, a duplicated pay summary and a duplicated
  acceptance sentence. Rich in covenants, sloppy in production — the same
  pattern as most of this corpus.
