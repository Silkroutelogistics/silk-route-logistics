# Arrive Logistics — Rate Confirmation, Arrive Order Number 535700

Retrieved 2026-08-14 from
`https://alpine.pairsite.com/yatruck/documents/2019/01/2019-01-04/1303/rate_sheet.pdf`
(an open document archive belonging to the carrier, YA TRUCK LLC). Live TMS
output, 2 pages. Sent 12/28/18 14:33. Pharr TX → Wilmer TX, 496 miles,
flat $1,000.00, declared value $100,000.00. Individual contact details redacted;
company names retained.

Arrive is the only broker in the corpus that puts its terms under a heading
called **OPERATIONAL RULES** and numbers them 1–12. It is also the only one
whose precedence clause resolves *in favour of the rate confirmation* rather
than the master agreement.

## OPERATIONAL RULES (pages 1–2, verbatim, numbered 1–12)

> 1. This Rate Confirmation is deemed accepted by Carrier **unless it is rejected within 48 hours of receipt.**
> 2. Receipt of shipment by Carrier constitutes acceptance of and agreement to the terms of this Rate Confirmation.
> 3. Double brokering without prior written authorization will result in forfeiture of payment by Arrive to Carrier.
> 4. **Any communication regarding this load must be addressed to Arrive and not its customer.**
> 5. All charges are included in this Rate Confirmation.
> 6. **Carrier must give Arrive notice 1 Hour prior to detention occurring.**
> 7. Carrier agrees in the event there are overages, shortages, or damages, Carrier will contact Arrive's office to report the discrepancy **before leaving the customer's premises.**
> 8. Payment will be made **within thirty (30) days after receipt of invoice, original BOL, and signed Load-Rate Confirmation** unless Arrive disputes the invoice or any part thereof.
> 9. Freight must not be handled or trans loaded by Carrier without approval from Arrive. In the event of Carrier's violation of this Operational Rule, **the limitation of liability as to cargo loss or damage set forth in the Broker Carrier Agreement between Arrive and Carrier shall be voided** and payment by Arrive to Carrier shall be forfeited by Carrier.
> 10. Carrier or its agent certifies that any Transportation Refrigeration Unit (TRU or reefer) equipment furnished will be in compliance with the in-use requirements of the California TRU regulations.
> 11. This Rate Confirmation incorporates the terms and conditions of a Broker Carrier Agreement signed by Arrive and Carrier.
> 12. **In the event of a conflict between this Rate Confirmation and any Broker Carrier Agreement between Arrive and Carrier, this Load-Rate Confirmation shall govern as to the provisions in conflict.**

Rule 12 is the important one. Every other document in this corpus that addresses
precedence resolves it the other way — Schneider, TQL, GlobalTranz, J.B. Hunt
and C.H. Robinson all make the master agreement control. Arrive makes the
per-load document control.

**This is directly relevant to a defect SRL already found and fixed.** Per the
corpus README, SRL's rate confirmation once said 48 hours while BCA §5 said 24,
*and the rate confirmation's own precedence clause made the BCA control* — so
the carrier was bound to 24 while reading 48. Under Arrive's Rule 12 the same
drafting accident would have resolved the opposite way: the carrier would have
been bound to what it could actually read. Neither ordering is inherently
correct, but Arrive's fails safer for the carrier and is worth surfacing when
the BCA/RC precedence question next reopens.

Rule 9's remedy is also distinctive: unauthorised trans-loading does not merely
forfeit payment, it **voids the cargo liability limitation in the master
agreement**. That converts a service breach into unlimited cargo exposure.

Rule 6's "1 Hour prior to detention occurring" requires the carrier to predict
detention an hour before it starts — a notice obligation that is arithmetically
awkward unless read as one hour before free time expires.

## HOS clause (page 1, verbatim)

> NOTE: By accepting this Rate Confirmation, Carrier warrants and agrees that it will follow all rules and regulations concerning its choice of driver(s), including assigning a driver who can perform the transportation services **without violating the Hours of Service of Drivers Regulations contained in 49 CFR 395** applicable at the time of acceptance of the shipment.

The only document in the corpus to cite 49 CFR 395 by number and to frame HOS
compliance as a **driver-assignment** warranty made at the moment of acceptance,
rather than as a general compliance covenant. That is a meaningfully different
allocation: the carrier warrants it picked a driver with hours, not merely that
it will obey the rules.

## Structural observations

- **"OPERATIONAL RULES" as a heading** is a better frame than "Terms and
  Conditions" for a per-load document. It signals what the list is for: things
  the driver and dispatcher must actually do, not risk allocation.
- **Twelve numbered rules on a two-page document** is the highest terms-density
  per page in the corpus, and it reads far more cleanly than GlobalTranz's
  single-paragraph wall carrying comparable content.
- **Rule 4 — talk to us, not our customer** — is a disintermediation guard that
  appears nowhere else in this corpus and is standard broker practice.
  Schneider says the same thing ("do not call the customer") but buries it in a
  numbered requirement about service failures.
- **48-hour rejection window** vs C.H. Robinson/MoLo's 24-hour objection window.
  Both are deemed-acceptance-by-silence; Arrive gives twice the time.
- Payment is **net-30 from receipt of invoice, original BOL, and signed rate
  confirmation** — the clock starts on the completeness of the packet, not on
  delivery. Same construction as TQL's "all pay terms are calculated from the
  day TQL receives your complete and legible paperwork."
