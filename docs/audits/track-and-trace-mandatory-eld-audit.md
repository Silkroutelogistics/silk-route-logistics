# Track & Trace — mandatory ELD / trailer-GPS tracking audit

**Date:** 2026-09-07 · **Baseline:** `main` at `172e6229` · **Status:** read-only audit, no code changed, no database touched. Web research for Part 2 was done with search and fetch only; every external claim there carries a URL.

**Policy under audit (verbatim from the brief):** *every SRL load must have live truck or trailer location from an ELD or trailer GPS API, tied to a named driver, verified as belonging to the assigned carrier, before that carrier can haul.* Highway is ruled out as the aggregator and is not proposed anywhere below.

**Method.** Part 1 started from `docs/architecture/track-and-trace-source-of-truth.md` and CLAUDE.md and was then verified line by line against source; three read-only sub-agent sweeps were run and every load-bearing claim they returned was re-checked by hand before it was written down (two of their claims were wrong and are corrected in place — noted where they occur). Line numbers are against `172e6229`. The three recurring bug lenses the brief asked for — status fields without enforcement, UI controls promising behaviour no code grants, and data populated but never consumed — are applied throughout Part 1 and gathered in §1.8.

---

## 0. The answers, up front

**What exists today, in one line:** SRL has **no live location source of any kind** — the Motive and Samsara adapters are real HTTP code but key off SRL's own single-tenant credentials that are set nowhere, the `eldService` the console and shipper portal actually read is simulated, no aggregator or trailer-GPS integration exists, and the one carrier-side path that could produce a genuine position (the driver ping link) is blocked twice over, by a verification step no UI can perform and by a global `Permissions-Policy: geolocation=()` header.

**Recommended insertion point:** **dispatch**, implemented as one shared "tracking-readiness" check that the three `DISPATCHED` writers and the rate-confirmation issue path all call — with the *fleet-level* half of the requirement (a verified, ownership-matched telematics connection) surfaced earlier as a Compass check and a compliance `blocked_code` so an unconnected carrier is visible from approval onward and cannot be tendered by auto-pilot. Reasons and the fallback for no-API providers are in §3.5.

**Top three risks:**

1. **The current Broker-Carrier Agreement promises the opposite.** F11 §22 says *"BROKER will not require CARRIER to integrate any particular ELD platform as a condition of doing business."* Carriers have signed that text. Enforcing a mandatory ELD gate before the BCA is amended and re-executed is a breach of SRL's own paper, and it also sits inside §10's no-control / no-coercion architecture (49 CFR 390.6), which counsel drafted deliberately.
2. **Coverage and the funnel.** SRL has one real carrier, PENDING, who has never logged in. A hard gate at onboarding on a base where a material share of small fleets run ELDs with no third-party API (or none at all — exempt fleets exist) turns a thin funnel into none. The fallback tier has to be designed, not improvised.
3. **Driver personal data, and the platform's own drift.** A named driver plus a live position is personal data under CCPA/CPRA (which covers employee data) and is federally the motor carrier's data under 49 CFR 395. And the codebase is not ready to carry it truthfully: the tracking-compliance factor is a constant 100 because `eldEnabled` has no writer, the ELD Compass check deducts 5 points from every carrier *with* trucks because `eldProvider` has no writer, and the shipper portal's "Last Known Position · via ELD" card is fed by a random-number generator. A mandate built on top of those without fixing them ships a lie with a signature under it.

## Part 1 — What is built

### 1.1 Reading the architecture document against the code

`docs/architecture/track-and-trace-source-of-truth.md` (v1.0, 2026-04-30) is still correct in its central claim — *"There is no GPS data today"* (§1) — and wrong in its explanation of why. It frames ELD as deferred until *"SRL acquires its own truck/driver"* (§4.1). The code that shipped after it tells a different story: two real ELD adapters, a driver-ping channel, a geofence engine and a telematics-activated Compass factor all exist, and none of them can produce a position. The document also still says the public tracking token is the load number (§3, §5.3); it is a 12-character `ShipperTrackingToken` since v3.8.aae, and `Load.trackingToken` is a UUID (`schema.prisma:1661`). It should be updated, but that is not this audit's deliverable.

### 1.5 The Compass Engine and the Compass Score

### 1.8 The three bug lenses, gathered

**Status fields without enforcement.** `eldDeviceVerified` / `eldOnFmcsaList` are written weekly and gate nothing. `eldEnabled` gates only a score, never a tender. `ELDDeviceMapping.isActive` is never consulted. The `LoadTrackingEvent` and `CheckCall` "last update" that the CRITICAL rule and the public tracker rely on is satisfied by a status click or the alert engine's own row, not by a position. `confirm-loaded` moves a load to `LOADED` with no state-machine call and no ownership check (§1.3). `broadcastTenderService` creates offers to N carriers with **no `complianceCheck`** (0 hits in the file); `instantBookService`, `fallOffRecovery.handleFallOffAcceptance` and the auto-match in `routes/automation.ts` all assign a carrier with no compliance check and no tender row (each file: `complianceCheck=0, assignCarrier=1, createTender=0`).

**UI controls promising behaviour no code grants.** The RC download 403 sends carriers to a My Loads control that does not exist. The shipper portal's "via ELD" card would render a random position. The integrations tiles imply a per-carrier connection where only a single-tenant env key exists; `dashboard/market/page.tsx:397` says ELD integrations *"will provide real-time GPS, HOS, and compliance data when connected."* The RC modal's "Terms & Conditions" textarea defaults to a clause 9 reading *"Carrier must provide tracking updates via GPS/ELD integration or manual check calls … Failure to provide timely updates may result in service penalties"* (`RateConfirmationModal.tsx:347`), but the field is `termsConditions` and the validator accepts only `customTerms`, so Zod strips it and the sentence **never reaches the PDF** (`RateConfirmationModal.tsx:311-316`, `pdfService.ts:2444`). The `eldRequired` option on the RC requirements block (`srl-chrome.ts:1776`, rendered as "ELD compliant" at `:1963`) is never set by `pdfService.ts:2302-2307`. The carrier compliance page filters Compass checks by the names `"ELD Validation"` and `"VIN Verification"` (`carrier/dashboard/compliance/page.tsx:234-238`); the backend name is `"ELD Device Verification"` and the VIN check no longer exists, so the ELD row never lands in the "Authority & Safety" bar.

**Data populated but never consumed, and its mirror.** Written-never-read: `ELDEvent` (no consumer beyond an AE JSON route with no frontend), `eldVerifiedAt`, `checkCallProtocol`, `driverConsentText`. Read-never-written: `eldProvider`, `eldEnabled`, `Load.originLat/destLat`, `Load.driverId/truckId/trailerId`, `Driver.currentLocation` and the HOS quartet, `CheckCallSchedule.MISSED`, `LocationSource.CHECK_CALL_EMAIL`, `ELDDeviceMapping.externalDriverId`, `urgencyLevel === "URGENT"`. Divergent identity: `ELDDeviceMapping.carrierId` is a `User.id` while `LoadTender.carrierId` is a `CarrierProfile.id` — the confusion that made waterfall accept silently dead for months (§13.3 Item 222.4), and a verification service must name which it means.

### 2.6 Driver-data privacy — citations for §4.3

- **CCPA/CPRA:** business thresholds (revenue >$26.625M from 2025-01-01, or ≥100,000 consumers/households, or ≥50% revenue from selling PI) — [CPPA](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/cpi_adjustment.html), [CA AG](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa); the employee and B2B exemptions **expired 2022-12-31** — [Morgan Lewis 2022-10-14](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2022/10/california-consumer-privacy-act-employee-and-b2b-exemptions-expire-january-1-2023); "precise geolocation" is *sensitive* personal information with a right to limit — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2022/02/cpra-employer-obligations); a sub-threshold broker can still be a service provider or third party under a covered carrier's or shipper's contract — [DWT contracting](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2019/05/practitioners-corner-ccpa-contracting-part-2).
- **Illinois BIPA:** biometric identifiers only (retina/iris, fingerprint, voiceprint, face/hand geometry), private right of action — [740 ILCS 14](https://law.justia.com/codes/illinois/chapter-740/act-740-ilcs-14/). Relevant to Compass check #22, not to location.
- **Texas, Colorado, Virginia** acts exclude individuals acting in an employment or commercial context — [DWT on TDPSA](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/07/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act-overview), [IAPP on Colorado](https://iapp.org/news/a/colorado-privacy-act-becomes-law), [NatLawReview on Virginia](https://natlawreview.com/article/comprehensive-data-privacy-law-brings-big-changes-to-virginia-excludes-employee); whether a 1099 owner-operator fits the employment carve-out is a counsel question.
- **Federal ELD rule:** §395.22 puts account management and record retention on the motor carrier, *"so as to protect a driver's privacy"* — [49 CFR 395.22](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/395.22); FMCSA FAQ: *"A motor carrier is free to use this data as long as it does not engage in harassment"*, *"Real-time tracking of CMVs is not required"*, FMCSA has no real-time access, and enforcement-transferred location is reduced to ~1-mile / ~10-mile precision — [FMCSA ELD FAQs](https://eld.fmcsa.dot.gov/FAQ/ShowAll), [final rule 80 FR 78292](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2015-12-16/html/2015-31336.htm). The rule is silent on sharing with brokers; custody sits with the carrier by practice, which is why the BCA has to state the authorization.
- **Provider-side limits** that shape what SRL can promise: Geotab Extendable Services withholds driver names — [Geotab](https://www.geotab.com/blog/extendable-services/); Trimble Trust Center scopes sharing per provider, truck and time — [Trimble](https://transportation.trimble.com/resources/blogs/trimble-trust-center-paving-the-way-to-better-visibility); FourKites CarrierLink states driver location is never shared with brokers — [FourKites](https://www.fourkites.ai/network/carrier-link-advanced-mobile).

### 3.1 The lifecycle as the code has it

**Load statuses** (`schema.prisma:71-90`): `DRAFT, PLANNED, POSTED, TENDERED, CONFIRMED, BOOKED, DISPATCHED, AT_PICKUP, LOADED, IN_TRANSIT, AT_DELIVERY, DELIVERED, POD_RECEIVED, INVOICED, COMPLETED, TONU, CANCELLED, PICKED_UP (legacy)`. **Tender statuses** (`:250-283`): `OFFERED, ACCEPTED, COUNTERED, DECLINED (carrier-initiated only), RC_SENT, CONFIRMED, EXPIRED, WITHDRAWN, RELEASED`. ### 3.4 Verification design — matching a feed to the carrier, the unit and the driver

**Identity keys first.** Any new service must say which id it means. `LoadTender.carrierId` and every Compass table key on `CarrierProfile.id`; `Load.carrierId` and today's `ELDDeviceMapping.carrierId` are `User.id`. The waterfall accept was dead for months on exactly this confusion (§13.3 Item 222.4). The connection record should be keyed on `CarrierProfile.id` and nothing else.

**Ownership (fleet ↔ carrier).** At connect time, read the provider's company object and store what it returns — Part 2 §2.4 records which providers expose a legal name and a USDOT — then match: USDOT must equal `CarrierProfile.dotNumber` exactly (it is FMCSA's identifier and Compass #1 has already verified it against SAFER); legal name is compared after normalizing case, punctuation and entity suffixes, with a DBA fallback from the SAFER record already fetched by `fmcsaService`; a DOT match with a name mismatch is a warning for the AE, a DOT mismatch is a hard fail. Store the provider's values verbatim on the connection row as evidence, the way `CarrierAgreement` stores IP and user agent — the question a dispute asks is "whose truck was that", and the answer has to be on the row, not re-derived.

**Unit (truck or trailer ↔ the load).** The RC already captures the unit number on paper. Make it data: at dispatch the assigned unit must resolve to a vehicle in the connected fleet by unit number or VIN (Motive's vehicle object carries `number`; Samsara's carries `name`; both carry an external id). Store the provider vehicle id on the load (`Load.telematicsUnitRef`), and poll **that unit only**, for **that load only**, from dispatch to POD — which fixes the design flaw in §1.2 row 3 where every truck's ping lands on every load. A trailer-GPS source binds the same way to `trailerNumber`.

**Driver (named ↔ the feed).** Three signals, any two of which suffice: the provider's current driver on the unit (Motive returns `current_driver` with name and phone, `motiveService.ts:44-49`; Samsara returns driver on the HOS endpoint) matches `Load.driverName` after normalization; the driver's phone was proven through the existing SMS step (`driverVerificationService`) — which then also mints the ping link as a secondary channel; or the driver exists on the carrier's Academy roster (`Driver.carrierProfileId`) with the same phone. Never treat a provider driver id as proof of a person — the codebase's own note (`driverVerificationService.ts:19-23`) is right that a verified phone proves a handset, not a CDL, and the same limit applies to a provider's driver record.

**Freshness.** "Live" needs a number. The engine's CRITICAL rule already uses 6 hours; a dispatch gate should use minutes (a position received since the RC was issued, and not older than, say, 60 minutes at dispatch), and the alert engine should keep the transit threshold. Whatever the numbers, put them in one constants module beside `RC_SIGN_SLA_HOURS`, not inline.

**Scope and evidence.** Read positions only for bound units while a load is between dispatch and POD; write every accepted position to `LoadTrackingEvent` with `locationSource: ELD` (already the adapters' convention) and the unit ref in `notes` or a new column; keep the raw provider payload in `ELDEvent` as it is today. The public tracker stays text-only (§14 PII scope, v3.8.ara).

### 3.5 Recommendation: gate at dispatch, surface at approval

**Recommended: dispatch, with the fleet-level half pulled forward.** Concretely — (a) a `CarrierTelematicsConnection` verified for ownership becomes a Compass check and a `complianceCheck` `blocked_code` (`TRACKING_NOT_CONNECTED`, overridable at first), so an unconnected carrier is visible from approval and cannot be placed by the waterfall or accept a board load without an AE's deliberate override; (b) the load-level requirement — unit bound, driver named and matched, position fresh — is a single `assertTrackingReady(loadId)` called by the three `DISPATCHED` writers and by RC issue, and it is what actually stops a truck rolling dark.

Evidence for that shape rather than the other two:

1. **The data the policy binds on does not exist before dispatch.** Driver and unit are null on four of the five accept paths (§3.2). A gate at acceptance can only enforce the fleet-level fact; a gate at onboarding cannot enforce any load-level fact at all. Only dispatch has all three.
2. **Dispatch is where the platform already puts its driver gate.** The RC is *"the document that sends a truck to a shipper"* and its download is already withheld until the handset is proven (`rateConfirmationController.ts:557-589`); the RC prints the driver/cell/truck/trailer blanks (`pdfService.ts:2200`) and its requirements block already carries a tracking-acceptance bullet (`srl-chrome.ts:1944-1957`). The policy is the same gate, made real.
3. **Onboarding as the hard gate kills the funnel it is meant to protect.** One real carrier, PENDING, never logged in (§0). A hard block at registration on a base that includes exempt and no-API fleets produces zero carriers, and the platform's own architecture memo says the Tier 2 ELD consent should be *"demand-driven, not speculative"* (`docs/internal/driver-sms-groundwork.md` §4). Surfacing the fleet-level fact at approval as a *visible* block that an AE can waive keeps the funnel while making the gap impossible to miss.
4. **It matches what the market actually does** — the large brokers require tracking before pickup/dispatch and use the carrier scorecard and load access as the lever, not registration (Part 2 §2.1).
5. **The write sites are countable.** Three `DISPATCHED` writers, one RC issue path, five accept paths that already share `complianceCheck`. A shared readiness check is the single-writer pattern this codebase has already adopted twice (`assignCarrier`, `settleTender`), and the `loadTenderWriters` / `carrierIdWriterDrift` guards show how to freeze the population afterwards.

**Roll out log-first, then enforce** (§13.3 Item 201's pattern): ship `assertTrackingReady` computing and recording a verdict for a full deploy cycle without blocking, read the counts, then flip.

**Fallback for carriers whose ELD provider exposes no API** (or exempt fleets with no ELD): they cannot meet the ELD/trailer-GPS clause literally, and the policy has to say what happens rather than leave it to the AE on the phone. Recommended tier, in this order of preference — (1) route through an aggregator that already holds an agreement with that provider (Part 2 §2.3 lists coverage; MacroPoint and Trucker Tools reach providers with no public API); (2) a **trailer-GPS** unit, which the policy already admits; (3) the platform's own **driver-ping link** at a fixed cadence (every 2 hours in transit plus geofence arrival/departure), already built, consented and phone-scoped — once the two blockers in §1.2 row 7 are removed — with mandatory phone verification before dispatch, a Compass consequence rather than a block, **exclusion from auto-pilot placement** (waterfall and bid-accept dispatch without a human, so they should not be offered to a carrier on the manual tier), and an AE-recorded exception on the load (`trackingMethod = DRIVER_PING`, reason, who approved) so the exception is a row and not a memory. What must not happen is silent SMS-only tracking: `DRIVER_SMS_CONSENT_TEXT` says *"Consent is not a condition of being dispatched"* (`driverVerificationService.ts:43`), and TCPA is why it says so, so the messaging channel cannot become the mandatory one — the provider feed is the carrier's consent, the ping is the driver's, and the fallback tier is the carrier choosing the second because it cannot offer the first.

### 4.3 Privacy — what this touches, flagged for counsel

- **Driver name + live position + mobile number is personal information**, and under CCPA/CPRA it has been in scope for employee and B2B data since 1 January 2023. SRL is unlikely to be a covered "business" on its own thresholds today, but a large carrier customer may be, and a broker receiving that carrier's driver data can be a service provider or a third party depending on the contract — §39 needs the corresponding terms. Other state acts largely exempt employee and B2B data, with variation; Part 2 §2.6 carries the citations.
- **ELD data is federally the motor carrier's.** 49 CFR Part 395 assigns the records to the carrier; nothing in FMCSA rules gives a broker any entitlement to them, so the whole mechanism rests on the carrier's authorization to its provider, and the BCA has to say exactly what was authorized. The FMCSA anti-harassment (49 CFR 390.36) and anti-coercion (390.6) rules are why §10's no-coercion text must survive verbatim — a mandatory feed plus Compass consequences plus check calls is the fact pattern those rules were written about, and the platform's own SMS consent already tells drivers messaging is not a dispatch condition.
- **The provider API returns more precision than the regulatory record.** The ELD rule reduces location precision on the RODS; provider telematics APIs return full-precision GPS from the device. The consent screen and §22.2 should describe the latter, because that is what SRL will hold.
- **Biometrics are already in play.** Compass check #22 "Biometric Facial Match" (`carrierVettingService.ts:589`) reads `facialMatchStatus`; Illinois BIPA exposure exists today independent of this policy and should go to counsel in the same pile.
- **Retention and the public page.** §39's "no longer than needed" needs a number once positions are stored at scale; the public tracker must stay coordinate-free (§14 PII scope, v3.8.ara) so a leaked BOL QR never becomes a live map of a named driver.
- **Consent architecture.** Two consents, two parties, two screens: the carrier authorizes the feed (commercial, B2B); the driver consents to messaging (TCPA, per handset, revocable by STOP). Collecting them in one flow or one sentence would make the second look like a condition of the first — the groundwork memo (§4.1) says the same.
